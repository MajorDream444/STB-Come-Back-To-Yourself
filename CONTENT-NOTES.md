# Flo Content Engine — Raw Reflection Over Performance

The goal is not to produce content.
The goal is to let people feel the truth of the process.

---

## The Natural Engine

Flo's content naturally flows from:

```
dream → insight → reflection → post
```

Not:

```
research → draft → edit → polish → post
```

---

## Core Principle

**Raw honest reflections are better than polished education content right now.**

People are not following Flo because she is an expert.
People are following Flo because they recognise something true in her.

Polish can hide that truth.

---

## What Works

**Voice notes while moving.**
Walking, cooking, cutting fruit — Flo's natural processing happens in motion.
Hit record. Say what you're noticing. That's a post.

**After-dream reflections.**
If something meaningful comes up in a dream, record it before the day takes over.
Dreams often surface what clients and sessions are already bringing up collectively.

**After-session insights.**
Something shifted in a session. Write three lines about what you noticed.
Not the client's story — your noticing.

**Honest observations.**
Something felt true today. Write it down in your own words.
No jargon. No teaching. Just honesty.

---

## What to Avoid

- Waiting for the perfect article before posting
- Trying to educate before you've connected
- Speaking as an authority when speaking as a human would land better
- Overexplaining breathwork or the nervous system
- Using words that sound professional but feel distant

---

## Content Formats That Feel Natural for Flo

| Format | Why it works |
|--------|-------------|
| Short voice note → transcribe → post | Captures real-time insight without overthinking |
| Walking video, unfiltered | Feels human, relatable, safe |
| Cooking / cutting fruit video | Shows Flo as a person, not a practitioner |
| Short written reflection (3–5 lines) | Instagram caption or Substack note |
| After-session journal (private or shared) | Compounds insights over time |

---

## Substack Direction

Substack can start as **personal journal reflections**, not formal essays.

The reader should feel like they are sitting across from Flo at a kitchen table,
not reading a newsletter from a wellness brand.

Topics that will likely resonate:
- What I noticed this week
- Something a client said that I haven't stopped thinking about
- What I had to learn by stopping, not doing more
- A dream I had and what I think it meant
- What safety actually feels like in the body

---

## The Rule

**Do not wait for it to be ready.**

The point is not to look polished.
The point is to let people feel the truth of the process.
