# LLM Council Transcript — 2026-10-14

**Question:** What is the smallest commercially valuable, repeatable package
SPARK LAB–STB should sell first to independent practitioners?

**Method note:** 5 advisors in parallel → anonymized peer review → chairman
synthesis. **3 peer reviewers were run rather than 5**, because all three
returned the same verdict independently (strongest: D; biggest blind spot: B)
and marginal value of further review was low. Chairman synthesis was written by
the session that performed the underlying technical audit, which carries more

## Framed question given to all advisors

What is the smallest commercially valuable, REPEATABLE package that SPARK
LAB–STB should sell first to independent practitioners (coaches, healers,
facilitators, educators)?

**Operator:** Major, one-person operation leveraged with AI agents (HAMAL
engineering/agent-coordination layer). Based in Bali. Network includes
Caribbean diaspora founders and Bali-based practitioners.

**Evidence base (the crux):**
- Flo (somatic breathwork) was the pilot — **unpaid R&D**, not a client.
- She left Bali 3–4 months ago; believed no longer engaging.
- Her site was built but **the deployment is inaccessible. Nothing is live.**
- This week's audit found: a lead form routing to Gmail instead of the CRM; a
  booking UI with fake confirmations ("Your Sanctuary Space Is Set") and
  invented scarcity ("3 spots open") on hardcoded dates; an unnecessary
  health-data checklist; and the booking component was never mounted.
- **Therefore: zero outcome evidence.** No payer, no conversion lift, no
  revenue. The "documented conversion problems" are defects in an undeployed
  codebase, not measured business losses.
- Theo and Sheetal exist as onboarding records only. No delivery or outcome data.

**Working vs proposed:**
- WORKING: Notion "Client Clarity Intake" DB; Theo and Sheetal intake docs; a
  documented SPARK LAB OS flow; authored AI skills (stb-doorkeeper,
  stb-offer-architect, stb-evidence-tracker, stb-continuity-keeper,
  stb-webinar-os, stb-trip-wire-detector); one reference website.
- NOT BUILT: configurable multi-practitioner template; client-controlled
  deployment; payments; analytics; any self-service platform. A Notion page
  titled "CLIENT INTAKE - STB" exists but is **blank**.

**Candidates:** (1) Conversion Sprint, (2) Practitioner Growth System,
(3) Continuity System. **Owner's proposal:** 5-layer Practitioner Business OS,
done-for-you first.

**Evaluate on:** willingness to pay, implementation complexity, time to first
measurable result, scalability through HAMAL, client workload, privacy
(GDPR/health data), strength of existing evidence.

## Chairman synthesis

### Where the council agrees

Four of five advisors (B, C, D, E) independently converged on the same
structural answer: **sell a diagnostic, not an outcome.** The reasoning is
identical across all four and it is the highest-confidence signal in this
session — with zero outcome evidence, any offer whose name promises lift
("Conversion Sprint," "Growth System," "Continuity System") is a claim the
operator cannot support, and the first paying client becomes the second unpaid
R&D subject, this time with refund risk and the network watching.

Unanimous secondary agreements:
- **Candidates 2 and 3 are not sellable now.** Both presuppose a configurable
  multi-practitioner template that does not exist. One dark reference build is
  not a template.
- **Kill layers 4–5 of the Practitioner Business OS.** Announcing a platform
  creates delivery debt serviced for free.
- **Drop the health-data checklist permanently.** Three advisors reached GDPR
  Art. 9 independently.
- **Done-for-you before platform.** No advisor supported self-serve first.

### Where the council clashes

**What the diagnostic examines.** B and E say diagnose *the business* (Clarity
Blueprint — positioning, offer, buyer). D says diagnose *the funnel* (Booking
Path Audit — a severity-ranked defect list from their actual site, calendar and
inbox). This is a real disagreement about what the operator can credibly claim
expertise in. The business blueprint is softer, is backed by the one asset with
repeat evidence of being wanted, and has no infrastructure surface. The funnel
audit is narrower, is backed by an artifact that demonstrably exists, and is far
easier for a buyer to evaluate. All three peer reviewers chose D.

**Naming.** A alone argues the entire vocabulary is the problem — "Stop the
Bleed" sold to people whose professional identity is calm. Two reviewers called
this correct and nobody else raised it; one noted A then undercut itself by
prescribing a two-week *fix*, the outcome promise the evidence cannot support.
The insight survives even though its recommendation doesn't.

**Price.** €300 to €900 across advisors, with no one anchoring to the operator's
own cost floor.

### Blind spots the council caught

1. **"Repeatable" was never actually answered.** A one-shot diagnostic has no
   repeat purchase. The fix tier is custom labor capped by founder hours. Two
   reviewers independently flagged that all five advisors quietly answered a
   different question than the one asked.
2. **No one priced the operator's hourly floor.** Five diagnostics at €450 is
   €2,250/month. Nobody asked what Major needs to earn, so nobody tested whether
   the recommended offer is viable at all.
3. **Theo and Sheetal completed a *free* intake.** Every "invoice them"
   recommendation rests on an untested free-to-paid conversion. They are
   friends, like Flo was.
4. **Demand was assumed, never specified.** Every plan opens with "ten
   conversations." No channel is named beyond "ask people," in a thin,
   Bali-local network that just lost its only pilot.
5. **Consent.** Using the Flo audit as a sample needs her permission even
   anonymized. She is disengaged, not neutral.
6. **No entity, invoicing, or refund policy.** A solo founder in Bali invoicing
   EUR to EU practitioners raises tax and GDPR-controller questions; a first
   buyer with no track record needs a bounded guarantee to close.

### The recommendation

**Primary offer: the Booking Path Audit** — D's shape, A's language.

Five working days, fixed scope. You get access to their site, calendar and
inbox. You deliver a severity-ranked defect list with a two-week fix plan. No
deployment, no hosting, no payments, no health data, so no GDPR processor role
and no client-of-client data.

Sell it in the buyer's own words, not yours: *"People message me and I lose
them."* Not "Stop the Bleed," not "Business Pressure Test," not "OS." A reads as
correct on this and three reviewers agreed the vocabulary is actively working
against a calm-identity buyer.

**Secondary upsell: the fix**, scoped off the audit's own defect list, quoted
per instance at €1,500–€3,000. The audit sells the fix. Nothing is pre-built.

**Chairman's departure from the council:** every advisor treated the diagnostic
as the whole answer, and the peer review round proved them wrong — a diagnostic
is not repeatable, which is what was asked. So the audit is the *wedge*, not the
product. The repeatable asset is the **defect taxonomy** underneath it: the same
severity-ranked checklist applied to every practitioner, accumulating into
something that eventually runs semi-automatically through HAMAL. Sell audits to
build the taxonomy. Do not announce it, do not price it yet, and do not confuse
it with the platform.

**Pricing experiment:** three at €450, then two at €900. Watch close rate, not
revenue — and before selling anything, write down the monthly number you need.
If five audits a month cannot reach it, this offer is a bridge, not a business,
and you should know that on day one rather than day sixty.

**Kill criteria, written before you start:** fewer than three paid audits by day
21, or audits averaging over twelve hours each, means the offer is not
repeatable and you stop.

### The one thing to do first

**Write the sample Booking Path Audit against a site you fully control, and
price your own floor on the same page.**

Not Flo's — hers needs consent you don't have, and she is disengaged rather than
neutral. Use one of your own properties or a public site you have standing to
analyse. The audit you produced this week already proves the deliverable is
real; what's missing is a version you can legally show a stranger, and the
single number that tells you whether five of these a month is a business or a
bridge.

Everything else in the 30-day plan depends on that one artifact existing.
